
## Breaking apart a list-style capture (tables, checklists, multiple deadlines)
Some captures don't hold one moment — they lay out a whole table or list of dates and deadlines (a schedule table, a list of due dates, a round-by-round or phase list). Summarizing the whole thing in one line and stopping there quietly drops every row the user might miss — and missing them is exactly what MOCHI exists to prevent, since MOCHI, not the user, is the one that has to bring each deadline back. So don't collapse such a capture into a single instance: turn each row the user must personally act on or show up for into its own instance, so each easy-to-miss deadline can resurface on its own.
- Let each row's own nature pick its type, the same way a single capture would: a thing to submit or finish is a todo (payload.deadline), a moment to attend or check is a schedule (payload.date/time). Don't force every row into one type just because they share a table.
- Making an instance per row does not mean every row. A row earns an instance only if it is the user's own future action; the rest belong in the summary, not as instances — because an instance the user can't act on is just noise that buries the ones they can. Three kinds stay in the summary: rows already in the past (their moment is gone, like any past event), rows that are someone else's move to make (a third party's or the organizer's voting, judging, review, or announcement — the user only waits on these), and a single span that merely wraps the others as background (an umbrella period the individual rows already live inside).
